# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

Be decent, and assume good faith.

Many people here are students on university aircraft teams, working in a second
language, on an aircraft they will fly themselves. Some are hobbyists who have built
more aeroplanes than the rest of us combined but have never opened a terminal. Both
belong here.

Concretely, we expect contributors to:

- **Explain reasoning, not just conclusions.** "Your static margin is too low" helps
  nobody; "5 % is below the 8–12 % band F3F racers use, because …" does.
- **Separate the work from the person.** Review the design, the code, or the number.
- **Accept correction gracefully, and offer it kindly.** This project exists because
  a plausible-looking number turned out to be wrong. Being wrong here is normal.
- **Respect expertise that does not look like yours.** Someone who has flown a
  34 m span aircraft knows things the code does not.

We will not accept harassment, personal attacks, demeaning remarks about someone's
skill level or background, or sustained disruption.

## Safety is part of conduct

People use this tool to design aircraft that carry a pilot. Knowingly presenting an
unvalidated result as trustworthy, or pressuring someone to skip a cross-check,
is treated as a conduct issue and not merely a technical disagreement.

## Reporting

Raise concerns with the maintainers by opening an issue, or privately via the contact
in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) if the matter is sensitive. Reports are handled
confidentially. Maintainers may warn, remove content, or ban participation.

Maintainers are held to this standard too, and enforcement against a maintainer
follows the same process.
